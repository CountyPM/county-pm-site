As a general matter, the step-up applies to the property regardless of the debt against it — so using a cash-out refinance to fund a gift does not, by itself, sacrifice that benefit. The basis adjustment attaches to the asset, not to your equity in it.

That is why the refinance-rather-than-sell approach appeals to owners who want to help a child now. Selling triggers gain and ends the hold. Borrowing against the property does neither: the proceeds are not taxable income to you, the property keeps compounding, and the basis treatment at death is generally unaffected by the loan sitting on it.

The caution is real, though, and it is not a formality. Estate and basis rules interact with your particular situation in ways that matter — how title is held, whether community property applies, what your total estate looks like, and what your state does. Those interactions can change the answer for a specific family even when the general principle holds. Rules in this area also change.

Confirm the specifics with your CPA before you act on the general principle, and do it before the refinance rather than after. This is general information, not tax or legal advice; confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
