At least 30 days for an increase of 10% or less, and at least 90 days for an increase above 10%. Civil Code §827 sets both periods, and the 10% test is measured against the rent charged at any time during the 12 months before the effective date — so it counts cumulative increases in that window, not just the one you are serving. Two 6% increases in the same year cross the line and the second one needs 90 days.

The notice period is independent of the rent cap, and that is the trap. AB 1482 governs how much you may raise; §827 governs how much warning you give. An increase can be comfortably inside the 5%-plus-CPI ceiling and still be void because it went out on 30 days when the cumulative math required 90. The two rules are checked separately, and a tenant contesting an increase will check both.

Service by mail extends the clock. §827 expressly makes the notice subject to Code of Civil Procedure §1013 when served by mail, which adds days to the period for mailing within California. Practically: count backward from the effective date, add the mailing extension, and then add a working margin. Keep proof of service with the tenant file, because a notice served improperly does not take effect no matter how correct the number in it is.

Exemption from the cap is not exemption from notice. An owner of a properly exempted single-family home or condominium can raise rent by any amount — and precisely because those increases are the ones most likely to exceed 10%, they are the ones most likely to need 90 days. Owners who have spent years thinking of themselves as outside AB 1482 are the most common source of defective notices in this county.

Local ordinances can add requirements on top. Oxnard and Ojai each run their own rent stabilization ordinances with their own caps, frequency limits, and notice forms. If your property is in either city, satisfy the ordinance and §827 both — meeting one does not excuse the other.

This is general information, not legal advice; notice rules and local ordinances change, so confirm the current requirements with a qualified professional before you serve an increase.
