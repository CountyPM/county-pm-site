You can, but it usually hurts both efforts. A for-sale sign discourages renters who want a stable 12-month lease, and the possibility of a tenant discourages owner-occupant buyers who want the home vacant. Most owners do better choosing one primary objective and switching only when a pre-set trigger is reached.

There is a practical wrinkle too: major listing sites generally show one version of a property at a time, so a for-sale listing has to come down before a rental listing will post, and if both exist the for-sale listing is the one that shows. Running both is rarely as efficient as it sounds.

The cleaner approach is sequential. List for sale with a defined deadline, then switch to renting if you hit the trigger. When you rent, write the lease around your exit: a term that ends before your target sale date.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice; confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
