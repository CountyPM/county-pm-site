It depends on your numbers, but holding is often cheaper than a large price cut when the rent covers most of the payment. At $500 a month of negative cash flow, it takes more than eight years to equal a $50,000 price reduction, and for a former residence the realistic comparison is roughly three years of holding costs ($6,000 a year, about $18,000) against the cut.

The price cut is certain and permanent. Holding is a bet on timing, and the bet has a deadline if the home was your residence, because the Section 121 tax exclusion generally disappears about three years after you move out. Holding also carries risks the cheap-looking $500 hides: a vacancy, a roof, or a water heater can turn it into much more, so you need a reserve before you choose it.

Taking the price cut can be the right call if you need the cash now, cannot fund a reserve, are near the end of your window, or simply do not want to be a landlord.
