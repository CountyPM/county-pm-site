Size it to real failures, not to a number of months. "Six months of expenses" is a comfortable-sounding rule that has almost nothing to do with what actually empties a reserve.

Build the number from the bottom instead. Price a roof, an HVAC replacement, a sewer lateral, a water heater and the flooring it ruins when it goes, and sixty vacant days — all using actual local costs, not national averages. Then set the reserve so you could absorb two of those events in the same twelve months. That combination is what forces most sales. Either event alone is survivable for almost everyone; the pair is what breaks people.

The second input is your own exposure, which is why the honest answer varies by owner rather than by property. A buyer whose income comes from the local real estate economy needs enough to survive a vacancy and an income interruption landing in the same quarter, because in that case the two events share a cause and will arrive together. A buyer with stable, documented income from a sector unrelated to housing can carry less, because those risks are genuinely independent.

So there are two questions, not one: what can this building cost me, and what happens to my income when it does. Reserve to the shape of your risk, and keep it somewhere you can actually reach in a week.
