No. Since AB 12 took effect on July 1, 2024, the small-landlord exception that lets an individual owner of two or fewer rental properties (four units or fewer total) charge up to two months' rent as a deposit expressly does not apply when the tenant is an active-duty service member — that tenant is capped at one month's rent no matter how small the landlord is or how the property is held.

There's no workaround by re-labeling: a "pet deposit," "move-in fee," or extra month collected up front doesn't change the analysis if the effect is charging a service-member tenant more than one month's rent to move in. The cap applies to the total held under Civil Code §1950.5, whatever it's called.

This matters for more owners than it might seem. In and around military communities — Naval Base Ventura County among them — a meaningful share of the local rental applicant pool is active-duty, so a landlord who's used to defaulting to the small-landlord two-month deposit needs an explicit check for service-member status before setting the deposit amount, not an assumption that the usual rule applies.

This is general information, not legal advice; confirming a tenant's active-duty status and applying the correct cap depends on your specific facts, so confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
