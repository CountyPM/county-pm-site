Rent remains owed while an eviction is pending, but nothing forces the tenant to actually pay it, which is why delay is so costly for owners. The tenant stays liable for the unpaid rent and for the reasonable rental value of the days after the notice expires, and the court can include those amounts in a judgment. Collecting them afterward is a separate problem and often a poor bet.

Be careful about accepting rent mid-case. Taking a payment after serving a three-day notice can be argued to waive the notice or reinstate the tenancy, which can force you to start over. Talk with your attorney before accepting or depositing any payment once a notice has been served.

The practical lesson is the same one we keep coming back to: every extra month of a contested case is a month of housing the owner is carrying. Act at the first missed payment, not the third.
