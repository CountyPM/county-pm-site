Substantially cheaper, and the gap is wider than the per-square-foot numbers suggest. In Ventura County, converting the inside of an existing garage tends to run somewhere around $150 to $220 per square foot, while new construction above a garage runs closer to $400 to $500. On 600 square feet that is roughly $120,000 against $350,000 for the same finished unit — before the structural work that building up actually requires.

The structural work is where the second option quietly loses. Garage slabs in California tract housing are typically about three and a half inches, unreinforced, with no vapor barrier, and were never engineered to carry a second story. Underpinning the slab or drilling piers to carry the new load commonly runs $25,000 to $60,000, and it is the kind of number you discover from a structural engineer after you are in escrow rather than from a listing photo before. Converting the interior adds no load anywhere new, so the foundation question simply does not arise.

Conversion has its own surprises, and they belong in the bid rather than in the change orders. Three recur: a topping slab or framed floor over the pitched, unreinforced garage slab, roughly $8,000 to $15,000; cutting the slab to trench kitchen and bath drain lines, roughly $15,000 to $25,000; and infilling the garage door opening with a header and shear wall, because the footing under a garage door opening usually is not sized to carry a bearing wall, roughly $6,000 to $12,000. Ask for those three as line items and you will learn quickly which contractors have done this before.

Build the carport anyway. State law does not let a city require replacement parking when a garage is converted in conjunction with an ADU, so you are not obligated — but a four-bedroom tract house with no covered parking takes an appraisal adjustment and narrows the pool of buyers at exit. On an existing driveway apron a carport runs roughly $15,000 to $30,000, which is cheap insurance against both.

All of these figures are illustrative and will move with your site conditions, your finish level, and who is bidding. Get bids from licensed, insured, bonded contractors, verify the license with the Contractors State License Board, and replace these numbers with yours. The ranking — conversion below new construction above a garage — holds much more reliably than any of the individual figures.
