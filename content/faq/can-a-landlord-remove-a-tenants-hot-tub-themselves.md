Generally, no — not on your own initiative, because a tenant's belongings, including a hot tub they installed, are their personal property, and hauling them off yourself is self-help that can expose you to a tenant-harassment or wrongful-eviction claim rather than solve your problem. Entry itself is also limited to the purposes and notice Civil Code §1954 allows; walking in to remove property doesn't fit neatly on that list.

A genuine, imminent safety emergency is different in kind from tidying up a lease violation. If a filled hot tub is actively about to collapse a balcony, draining it to stop that specific emergency is a narrower act than removing and disposing of the tenant's property, and entry for an emergency is one of the purposes §1954 does allow. Even then, do only what the emergency requires and document it immediately — photos, time, what you did and why.

The lease violation itself gets handled through the process described in the eviction entries on this hub: notice to cure, an opportunity for the tenant to remove it, and escalation only if they refuse. That's slower than doing it yourself, but it's the path that doesn't hand the tenant a harassment or self-help eviction claim against you.

This is general information, not legal advice; what counts as a true emergency justifying entry is fact-specific, so confirm your situation with a qualified professional before acting.
