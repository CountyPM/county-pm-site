Much less than a filled hot tub actually weighs, which is exactly why the combination is dangerous rather than merely against the rules. A hot tub loaded with water and people can concentrate two tons or more onto a small footprint — often double or more of what an ordinary residential balcony is designed to carry, and residential balconies are engineered for people and furniture spread across the whole surface, not thousands of pounds on a few square feet in one corner.

No number in this answer, or in any general guide, tells you what a specific balcony can safely hold — that's a question for a structural engineer looking at that balcony's actual construction, span, and condition, not a rule of thumb. If you discover a tenant has put something this heavy on a balcony, deck, or patio, treat it as a safety issue first: get it assessed and, if there's genuine risk, addressed immediately, before you deal with it as a lease violation.

That's the practical reason to have — and enforce — the alterations clause described elsewhere in this hub before this ever happens on your property, rather than finding out about the weight problem after the fact.
