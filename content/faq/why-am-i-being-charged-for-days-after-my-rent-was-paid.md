Because in California a tenancy ends when you legally surrender possession, not simply on the last day your rent was "paid through." If you keep the keys or haven't fully moved out past that date, the unit still isn't available to the landlord, so rent can continue to accrue for those extra days.

The charge reflects continued possession, not a penalty. A periodic tenancy ends according to the proper termination notice under Civil Code Section 1946.1, and any prorated rent and the 21-day deposit clock run from when possession is actually surrendered.

The practical takeaway: to stop the meter, return possession completely, keys included, by the date you intend to be done. For Ventura County tenants and owners, aligning the notice date, the move-out, and the key handoff avoids exactly this kind of surprise. Confirm your situation against Civil Code Sections 1946.1 and 1950.5 or with counsel.
