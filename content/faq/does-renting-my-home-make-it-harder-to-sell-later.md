It can, if the lease is not set up with the sale in mind. Renting first is not a problem in itself; a poorly timed lease is. Match the lease term to your planned sale date so the tenancy ends before you want to list, because a tenant in place narrows your buyer pool and limits showings.

The second piece is California's exemption notice. A single-family home owned by an individual can be exempt from the statewide rent cap and just-cause eviction rules, but only if the lease includes the required written exemption notice. Without it, the home is generally covered, and wanting to sell is not a valid reason to end the tenancy. The notice requirements and the exemption conditions are in Civil Code Sections 1947.12 and 1946.2.

Finally, a vacant, prepared home usually sells better than one with a tenant in place. If a sale is the goal, build the exit into the lease from day one: a defined end date, the exemption notice, and a plan for the handoff.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice; confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
