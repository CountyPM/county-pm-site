Yes, you can sell a house that has a tenant in it, but the sale works differently. A signed lease generally stays in force after the sale, so the buyer takes the property subject to the tenant's rights. That shifts your likely buyers toward investors and away from owner-occupants, who typically want the home vacant.

California also requires written notice before you enter for showings, normally 24 hours under Civil Code Section 1954, and a tenant who is cooperative makes a better showing. Wanting to sell is generally not, by itself, a legal reason to end a tenancy early, so plan the lease around the sale. If the home is covered by the statewide just-cause rules, a sale is not a listed ground, while an individually owned single-family home can be exempt only if the lease carries the required exemption notice.

Many owners find the simplest path is to set the lease term to end at or before the planned listing date, then sell the home vacant and prepared. One more date to watch: if the home was your residence, the Section 121 exclusion generally requires a sale within about three years of moving out.
