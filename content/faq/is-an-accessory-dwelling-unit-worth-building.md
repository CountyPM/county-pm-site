      The last paragraph above says the rent-cap exemption is "worth
      confirming." That is too soft. The AB 1482 exemption does not run to
      single-family homes as a category — it runs to property alienable separate
      from the title to any other dwelling unit. State law requires a recorded
      deed restriction prohibiting separate sale of a junior accessory dwelling
      unit, running with the land. The day that restriction records, the parcel
      generally stops being separately alienable and the exemption goes with it,
      on the main house too. So the real trade is a permanent, unconditional
      exemption on one unit in exchange for two or three capped units at roughly
      twice the gross. Most Ventura County parcels still clear that trade — a
      downstairs bedroom with its own bath converted to a JADU can run around
      $1,800 in permit-and-conversion cost where the plumbing is already
      stubbed, and AB 1154 (approved October 10, 2025) narrowed the JADU
      owner-occupancy requirement so it applies only when the junior unit shares
      sanitation facilities with the main house, which is what makes this
      workable for an absentee owner at all. As of 2026-09-07: price the
      exemption as a line item in the model, not a footnote, and confirm with
      counsel exactly when it drops for your parcel — it is often earlier than
      owners assume.

      different currencies. An existing owner gets Proposition 13: converting
      reassesses the new construction only, so on a house carrying a $400,000

It comes down to construction cost and how a lender treats the finished product — not to whether you can get it approved. Entitlement used to be the obstacle in California and largely is not anymore; state law has pushed hard in the other direction. That changes which questions actually decide the deal.

What makes an accessory dwelling unit distinctive is that it raises a property's income without raising the property count. No second purchase, no new loan qualification, no new market to learn, no additional set of local rules to master. For an owner who already has the land and the relationship with the neighborhood, that is a genuinely different proposition than buying another house.

The number that decides it is cost per square foot against the rent the finished unit will actually command in that specific neighborhood — not the county average and not what the ADU builder's brochure assumes. Get real bids, plural, and add the items that are not in the bid: utility connections, site work, permits and impact fees, and the months of carrying cost while it is under construction.

The financing question is the one people skip. Ask a lender in advance how the unit will be treated on a future refinance or sale — whether the rental income counts toward qualifying, and how an appraiser in your area is valuing ADUs today. An ADU that adds income but appraises for very little changes what the project is worth to you.

One California-specific caution: adding a second unit can affect whether your property still qualifies for the single-family exemption from the statewide rent cap. That is worth confirming for your specific parcel before you break ground, because it changes how you can operate the main house afterward.
