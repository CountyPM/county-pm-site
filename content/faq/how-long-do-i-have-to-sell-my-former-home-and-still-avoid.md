Generally about three years from the day you move out. To exclude up to $250,000 of gain from tax (up to $500,000 for married couples filing jointly), you must have owned the home and lived in it as your main home for at least two of the five years before the sale. Once you move out and rent it, that five-year lookback keeps running, so the exclusion is available only if you sell while you can still show two years of residence in it.

Miss the window and the exclusion is gone, and on a home with large gain the combined federal and California tax can approach the size of a price cut you were trying to avoid. Two caveats: depreciation you claimed while renting is taxed on sale even when the rest of the gain is excluded, and a 1031 exchange is a separate long-term path for property that has truly become an investment, not a way around the deadline.

Set the sale date, and the date your lease ends, before you rent the home out. Tax rules like this depend on your facts, so have your CPA confirm the dates.
