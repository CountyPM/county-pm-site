A clear alterations clause, written before you need it rather than drafted in response to one tenant's hot tub. It should prohibit structural changes, electrical or plumbing work, and any object over a stated weight on a balcony, deck, or patio without the owner's prior written consent — naming a specific weight limit, not just "heavy items," is what makes the clause enforceable rather than debatable.

The clause won't stop every tenant from doing something unauthorized, and it's not meant to. What it does is convert an ambiguous dispute — was this okay or not? — into a documented lease breach the moment it happens, which is what lets you use the three-day notice-to-cure process described elsewhere on this hub instead of arguing habitability and reasonableness from scratch.

Pair the clause with move-in and periodic inspection photos of balconies and decks so you can show what was there before, and you've built the same kind of record that helps in the house-number and security-deposit disputes covered in this hub — proof created before there was any dispute to prove.
