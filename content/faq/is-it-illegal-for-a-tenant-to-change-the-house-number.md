Very likely yes, but that doesn't get the lockout done that day, which is why it happens anyway. The California Fire Code, adopted by cities and counties throughout the state, requires address numbers to be plainly legible and visible from the street fronting the property — removing or obscuring them is a code violation on its own, independent of any eviction. Many local ordinances layer additional address-posting requirements on top of the state fire code.

Deliberately misleading a sheriff's deputy carrying out a lawful writ can also expose the tenant to obstruction exposure, separate from the code violation. Neither of those is a fast remedy, though — a fire-code complaint or a report to the sheriff's civil unit doesn't reverse the delay in the moment; it's evidence for later, not a lockout that happens today.

That gap between "this is against the rules" and "this fixes today's lockout" is the entire reason the tactic works. The landlord's actual remedy is proving the address on the spot (see the next answer) and rescheduling — the code violation is worth documenting for the record, not for same-day relief.

This is general information, not legal advice; enforcement of address ordinances and any obstruction exposure depends on your city and the specific facts, so confirm with a qualified professional.
