A California residential tenancy ends when the resident surrenders possession — the unit is fully vacated and all keys are returned — not simply when rent is "paid through" a certain date. Paying through the end of the month shows how far you are paid up; it does not terminate the tenancy on its own.

Legally, a periodic tenancy ends on proper notice and surrender of possession under Civil Code §1946.1, which requires 30 or 60 days' notice depending on how long the resident has lived there. As long as the resident keeps the keys and access — even if they are only returning to clean — the tenancy is still active, and rent can continue to accrue.

That surrender date matters for both sides: it is also when the 21-day deposit clock under §1950.5 begins. For a clean Ventura County move-out, vacate, return every key, and provide a forwarding address on the same day. Confirm the notice specifics against the statutes for your tenancy.
