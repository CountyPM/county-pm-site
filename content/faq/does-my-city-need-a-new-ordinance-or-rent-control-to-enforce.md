No. The Tenant Protection Act is state law, and since SB 567 took effect on April 1, 2024, the authority for a city attorney or county counsel to enforce it comes directly from that state statute — a city does not need its own rent-control ordinance, a local just-cause law, or any additional local legislation to act on TPA violations within its borders.

What a local ordinance or a dedicated reporting program does is make enforcement easier to trigger, not legally necessary. A city that sets up a webpage for tenants to report suspected violations, the way Anaheim has, is simply routing complaints to the city attorney's office faster — it isn't creating new authority that wasn't already there.

The upshot for owners: assuming your city is "hands-off" on rent regulation because it has no local rent-control ordinance is the wrong read. If your rental is subject to the TPA's statewide cap and just-cause rules — which most residential rentals over 15 years old are, subject to the usual exemptions — that city's attorney can act on a violation whether or not the city has ever passed a rent-control law of its own.

This is general information, not legal advice; whether a specific rental is TPA-covered depends on ownership structure, age, and use, so confirm your exemption status with a qualified professional.
