Bring paper that ties the physical property to the writ, not just your word. Dated photos showing the original house number in place (ideally from the move-in inspection, so the date is unimpeachable), a printed Google Street View image of the address, the county assessor's parcel map, and the addresses of the properties on either side all help a deputy confirm the match in the field instead of just taking your assertion.

Bring a key that fits the lock, too — it's a small thing, but it's more evidence that you're the party who's supposed to be there. And if you have any reason to suspect a tenant might try tampering with the address before lockout day, call the sheriff's civil unit ahead of time and flag it; a deputy who's already been told to expect a mismatched number is far less likely to simply walk away when they see one.

None of this is required paperwork for a normal lockout — it's insurance against the one day it goes sideways. Owners who've been through a contested lockout once tend to bring this file every time after.
