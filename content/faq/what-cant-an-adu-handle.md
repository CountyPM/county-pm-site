Skilled nursing. An accessory dwelling unit cannot deliver continuous medical care, and it cannot manage a serious overnight fall risk — there are conditions where a licensed facility is the only appropriate answer, and no amount of thoughtful construction changes that. Be honest about it at the planning stage rather than after the unit is built, because the version of this decision families regret is the one where the ADU was implicitly treated as a substitute for care it was never going to provide.

What the arrangement handles well is the long middle. Most of what an aging parent actually needs for years before any of that is help with driving, meals, medication, paperwork, and the ordinary friction of running a household alone — the same list that families end up paying a facility a great deal of money to provide. A unit twenty feet away covers that list at close to zero marginal cost, and it covers it without moving someone away from their neighbors, their church, and the grocery store they know.

Design for the constraint you can see coming. A converted garage is the only unit in the usual ADU menu that is inherently single-story, which matters more over a ten-year horizon than almost anything else on the plan: no stairs, a walk-in shower, a wider doorway, a small footprint that one person can keep clean. That is worth choosing deliberately rather than ending up with by accident.

And plan the exit before you need it. The realistic sequence is that the unit serves the long middle and then, at some point, does not — at which point it converts to a rental, houses another family member, or supports a sale. An ADU built to be habitable by a stranger is also the one that adapts; an ADU built as a custom annex for one person is a harder asset to redeploy at the exact moment the family has less capacity to deal with it.

This is general guidance about a decision that is as much a family question as a property one. Care needs are individual — talk to the physicians and, where money or title is involved, to an attorney and a CPA before committing to the construction.
