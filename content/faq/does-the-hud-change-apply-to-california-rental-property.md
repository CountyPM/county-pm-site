Not in any way that helps an owner. HUD's May 22, 2026 enforcement memo governs only how the federal agency handles Fair Housing Act complaints — it does not, and cannot, change California law. California defines assistance animals to include untrained support animals, and the California Civil Rights Department enforces that standard on its own authority, independent of anything HUD does.

The practical picture for a Ventura County rental: the federal complaint route for untrained emotional support animals has effectively closed, but the state route is fully open, free for the tenant to use, and built on a standard that never required training in the first place. Private lawsuits under the Fair Housing Act also remain available regardless of HUD's enforcement posture.

The trap is acting on the headline. An owner who denies a support animal because he read that "HUD gutted ESA protections" has not reduced his exposure — he has created a documented, dated decision under a state law that still protects the animal. The denial itself is rarely the problem; the paper trail explaining it usually is. Run the same individualized process you ran before May 2026: verify the documentation against California's standard, respond in writing, and deny only on evidence about this specific animal's conduct.

This is general information, not legal advice; confirm your obligations under California law with the Civil Rights Department guidance above or with counsel before acting on any federal policy change.
