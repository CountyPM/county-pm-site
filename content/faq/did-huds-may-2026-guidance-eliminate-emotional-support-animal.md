No. HUD changed how it will enforce complaints, not what the law requires. On May 22, 2026, HUD's Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity issued an enforcement memorandum saying it will pursue animal-related accommodation cases only where the animal is trained to provide disability-related assistance — following its September 2025 rescission of the 2013 and 2020 assistance-animal guidance. In practice, new federal complaints over denial of an untrained emotional support animal will be dismissed or receive a no-cause finding.

But the Fair Housing Act itself was not amended, no court invalidated it, and private lawsuits remain available — generally within two years. An agency deciding not to pursue a category of complaint is not the same thing as the underlying conduct becoming lawful.

For California owners the change matters even less. California's housing regulations define assistance animals to include support animals, which do not need to be trained or certified, and the California Civil Rights Department enforces that standard independently of HUD. A tenant whose support animal request is denied doesn't need the federal agency — they can file with CRD at no cost, under a standard that never had a training requirement. Denying an untrained ESA in California carries the same state-law exposure after the memo as before it.

This is general information, not legal advice; fair-housing rules carry real liability, so confirm your situation with the Civil Rights Department guidance above or with a qualified professional.
