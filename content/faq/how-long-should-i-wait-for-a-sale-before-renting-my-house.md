Set the deadline before you list, not after the house has sat for months. Base it on typical days on market for your price range and neighborhood plus a cushion, pair it with a monthly carrying-cost limit, and switch to renting at whichever point comes first.

Deciding in advance matters because the alternative is drift. Each week feels like it might be the week an offer arrives, and the carrying costs keep running. If the home was your residence, the clock is also tax-driven: the Section 121 exclusion generally requires a sale within about three years of moving out, so renting buys time but not unlimited time.

When you hit the trigger, switch cleanly. Take the house off the market, prepare it for a long-term lease, and set a lease end date that fits your sale plan. Most owners do better picking one primary objective at a time than listing for sale and for rent together.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice; confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
