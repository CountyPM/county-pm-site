Ten court days after being served with the summons and complaint, not counting weekends and court holidays. California extended the response period from five to ten court days for eviction cases filed after the 2024 change took effect, so older articles that say five days are out of date.

If the tenant files a written response within that window, the case is contested and moves toward a trial date. If the tenant does not respond in time, the landlord can ask the court to enter a default and proceed to judgment. How the days are counted matters: the clock starts on valid service, and a defect in service can restart it.

Owners should build that ten-court-day period into their timeline. It sits after the three-day notice period and before any trial, and it is one of several steps that make even a simple nonpayment case take longer than most owners expect.
