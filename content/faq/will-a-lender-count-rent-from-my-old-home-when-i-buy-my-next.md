Often partly, yes. Many lenders will count a portion of the rent from a signed lease toward qualifying for your next loan, rather than counting the full payment on the old home as a burden. The percentage they credit and the paperwork they require vary by lender and loan type, so this is a conversation to have before you rent the old home out, not after.

Ask your lender early: how much of the rent they will count, whether they need a signed lease and a deposit on file, how many months of landlord history or reserves they want, and what they require if the home is your departing residence. Getting those answers first lets you structure the lease and the timing so it helps your purchase instead of surprising you at underwriting.

The old house paying most of its own costs also makes a move-up less risky, since you are not carrying two full mortgages while it sits empty.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice; confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
