Only if the trust actually owns a small enough portfolio — the trust form itself doesn't disqualify it, but it doesn't help it either. Since AB 12 took effect on July 1, 2024, the general statewide cap on security deposits is one month's rent. The small-landlord exception that allows up to two months' rent is limited to an individual person or a single-member LLC who owns no more than two residential rental properties totaling no more than four units combined — and the statute treats the settlor or beneficiary of a family trust as that individual owner, so holding the property in trust doesn't by itself break the exception.

What breaks it is scale. A family trust that has accumulated a portfolio of rental homes over the years — which is exactly the situation many inherited or long-held family properties end up in — will almost always exceed the two-property, four-unit limit and fall back to the one-month cap regardless of the trust structure. Count every residential unit the trust holds anywhere, not just the one you're leasing right now.

One more limit applies no matter how small the ownership: the small-landlord exception never applies when the tenant is an active-duty service member. That tenant is capped at one month's deposit regardless of who owns the property or how many units they hold.

This is general information, not legal advice; whether your trust's holdings qualify depends on an accurate count across all its properties, so confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
