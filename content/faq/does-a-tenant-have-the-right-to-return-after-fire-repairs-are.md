Yes. Under California's SB 610, once remediation is complete the landlord must notify the tenant that the unit is habitable again, and the tenant holds a right to return to it at the pre-disaster rent.

The owner cannot treat the repair as a chance to reset the tenancy. Re-leasing the restored unit to a new tenant at a higher rent instead of bringing the displaced tenant back is exactly what the law is designed to prevent.

This pairs with SB 610's other disaster protections: rent stops during a mandatory evacuation, prepaid rent is refunded, and the owner is responsible for cleaning up disaster debris before the unit is considered habitable.
