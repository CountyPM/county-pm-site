Its buyer pool is the widest and the least discretionary. That combination is what keeps modest properties trading when higher-end product stops.

The people buying entry-level homes are renters aging into ownership, and they are moving for reasons that do not pause for market conditions — getting married, having a child, needing a yard, being finished with apartment living. Those are life events on their own schedule. A buyer in that position is comparing a mortgage payment to the rent they are already paying, not deciding whether now is a clever moment to deploy capital.

Higher-end buyers can simply wait, and in a soft market most of them do. They already own something adequate, their move is discretionary, and waiting costs them nothing. So that segment goes illiquid first: days on market stretch, price reductions accumulate, and the pool of qualified buyers thins at exactly the moment a seller needs it.

For an owner, this is a liquidity argument rather than an appreciation argument. Entry-level property may not appreciate faster, but it stays sellable — which matters enormously in the one scenario where it counts, when you need to exit and the market is not cooperating. It is the same reason that product also rents more reliably.
