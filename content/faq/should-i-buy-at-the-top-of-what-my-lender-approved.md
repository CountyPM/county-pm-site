No. Treat the approval as a ceiling you deliberately stay under, not a target to hit. The gap between what you qualify for and what you actually buy is not wasted money — it is the thing that funds your reserve, absorbs a bad year, and leaves you able to buy again.

Buying at the top of the approval means every subsequent problem has to be solved out of income you have already committed. A water heater that ruins the flooring, a two-month gap between jobs, an insurance renewal that jumps — each of those is manageable with margin and destabilizing without it. Almost every forced sale we see traces back to a payment that was sized to the best case.

A practical way to hold the line is to buy at a price where the numbers would still work if the property had to be rented. That single test does a lot of work. It caps the payment at something the local rental market can support, it keeps your options open if your life changes, and it quietly steers you away from the property that only makes sense if you personally live in it forever.
