In most cases the deposit stays with the tenancy and is not refunded piece by piece when one roommate leaves. The departing roommate's share is settled between the roommates, often by the incoming roommate paying the person who is leaving, and the full deposit is accounted for when the tenancy as a whole ends.

The deposit is held for the tenancy, not in separate envelopes for each person. Roommates frequently split it unevenly at move-in, or one person fronted it and was paid back by the others, and the landlord usually has no way of knowing. At County Property Management we deliberately stay out of who paid what. Tying the deposit to the tenancy, rather than to individuals, keeps the owner out of disputes between roommates and keeps the accounting clean.

When the last tenants eventually move out, California Civil Code §1950.5 governs the return: the landlord has 21 days after the tenants vacate to return the deposit or send an itemized statement of deductions. Any roommate change mid-tenancy should be papered with a signed amendment so everyone knows the deposit carried forward.
