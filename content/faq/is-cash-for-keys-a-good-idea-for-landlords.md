Sometimes it is the cheapest option, but mostly because court delay makes it so. When a contested eviction could cost months of unpaid rent plus attorney fees, paying a tenant to leave by a set date can limit your total loss and get the unit back sooner.

If you do it, do it carefully. Use a written, signed agreement that sets a specific move-out date and condition of the unit, pay only when the keys are returned and the unit is empty, and have an attorney look at the agreement if the tenancy is covered by California's just-cause rules, since some situations carry notice or relocation obligations. Do not accept rent or make promises that could undermine a notice you have already served without advice.

Cash for keys is a tool, not a strategy. The better fix is prevention: careful screening on the way in and early action on the first missed payment.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice; confirm your situation with a qualified professional.
