No — not in California. An emotional support animal is an assistance animal under California's Fair Employment and Housing Act and the federal Fair Housing Act, not a pet, so a housing provider must consider it as a reasonable accommodation even under a no-pet policy. Breed, size, and weight restrictions don't apply, no pet fee can be charged, and rejecting a qualified applicant who has legitimate documentation is housing discrimination.

The May 2026 federal change did not alter this. On May 22, 2026, HUD's fair-housing office issued an enforcement memorandum saying it will pursue animal-accommodation complaints only where the animal is trained to provide disability-related assistance, after rescinding its older assistance-animal guidance in September 2025. That narrowed federal enforcement — it did not amend the Fair Housing Act, and it has no effect on California's standard, which never required a support animal to be trained. The California Civil Rights Department enforces the state rules independently of HUD, and private lawsuits remain available. A denial that was unlawful in California before the memo is still unlawful after it.

What you can do is insist the documentation meets California's standard. Under AB 468 (effective January 2022), an ESA letter must come from a licensed professional who established a client relationship at least 30 days before issuing it and completed a clinical evaluation; an online certificate with no individualized assessment is presumptively not reliable third-party information. Deficient paperwork supports a written request to cure — not an automatic denial.

A denial holds up only on narrow grounds: this specific animal poses a direct threat to health or safety, or would cause substantial physical damage, shown by that animal's documented conduct. Breed reputation is not evidence. The tenant remains responsible for the animal's behavior and for any actual damage it causes. For applicants, the practical advice runs the other way: disclose the animal up front with documentation in hand — concealing it and producing paperwork only after a problem surfaces damages both the tenancy and the owner's trust. Owners should weigh a qualified ESA applicant on their full profile: payment history, employment, references.

This is general information, not legal advice. Fair-housing rules carry real liability, so verify a specific situation against the Civil Rights Department guidance above or with counsel before denying any accommodation request.
