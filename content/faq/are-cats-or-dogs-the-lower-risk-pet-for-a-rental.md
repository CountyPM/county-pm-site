In our experience, cats are generally the lower-risk pet. Most cats use a litter box by instinct, so the everyday wear is small. The real cat risk is spraying, which is territorial marking, mostly by male cats and far more common in cats that haven't been neutered. A spayed or neutered, indoor-only cat is usually an easy yes.

With dogs, the risk depends on the individual animal more than the species. Chewing and urine damage cluster at both ends of a dog's life: puppies chew and have accidents until they're trained, and senior dogs can lose bladder control near the end. A mature, house-trained adult dog with a good reference from a prior landlord is often as safe as a cat.

So the better question isn't cat or dog, but which animal. Ask the age, ask whether it's spayed or neutered, ask how a dog was trained and whether a cat goes outside, and talk to the last landlord. Then cover the remaining risk with pet rent, a pet addendum that spells out move-out responsibilities, renter's insurance with liability coverage for dogs, and periodic inspections.
