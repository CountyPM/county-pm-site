Mostly because homes here cost far more than local wages can support, and new supply is limited. With a median existing single-family price around $937,500 in mid-2026, many young couples who grew up here, along with teachers, nurses, and the children of longtime residents, compete for a small stock of homes against buyers with decades of equity.

Some respond by renting longer than they planned. Others buy farther out, often in Santa Clarita or beyond, and commute back to jobs in the county. A growing number leave California entirely for states where a paycheck still buys a house.

For owners this matters: the departure of younger households weakens the long-term pool of buyers and workers, while the same scarcity keeps rental demand strong. This is our reading of the trend, not a census count, so check current data before making a large decision.
