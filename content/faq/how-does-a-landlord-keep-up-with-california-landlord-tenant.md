Treat currency as its own discipline, on a calendar, separate from running the property. No agency notifies you when a requirement changes. A lease clause or notice form that was correct for twenty years does not update itself, and nothing in your file will look wrong on the day it stops being sufficient.

The practical version is an annual review. Once a year — late fall is the natural time, since most California housing legislation takes effect January 1 — pull your notice templates, your lease, your move-in condition report, and your move-out procedure, and have counsel or your property manager confirm what changed and what has to change in response. That is a short list of documents and a couple of hours, not a research project.

Do not try to keep up by memorizing the current checklist. The rules have changed structurally more than once in the last few years and they will change again, and a memorized list quietly goes stale. What survives amendments is the underlying habit: create each document at the moment it is supposed to be created, because the timing is part of the evidence and cannot be reconstructed later.

The other half of the answer is where you get your information. Owner forums and vendor newsletters run months behind and often describe a bill that never passed. Go to the statute, a California Courts self-help page, or a person who is professionally responsible for being right about it.
