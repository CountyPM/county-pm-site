Yes. The statewide security deposit cap applies to everything you hold, however it's labeled — a "pet deposit," a "cleaning fee," or last month's rent collected up front all count toward the same total, so calling one part of the money something else doesn't create room to collect more than the cap allows. One month's deposit plus a separate pet deposit plus last month's rent is, functionally, a multi-month collection wearing three different names.

Assistance animals are a distinct rule, not a version of the pet-deposit rule: no pet deposit, pet rent, or pet fee of any kind may be charged for a service or emotional support animal, because it's a reasonable accommodation under fair housing law rather than a pet. Treating an assistance animal like a pet for deposit purposes is itself a fair-housing problem, separate from and in addition to any deposit-cap issue.

If you want deposit money earmarked for potential pet damage, build that into the single total deposit you're already charging under the cap rather than adding a second, separately labeled collection on top of it.

This is general information, not legal advice; how a specific fee is characterized can matter, so confirm your lease structure with a qualified professional.
