Margin. Everything else on the list is a version of it.

Buying under what you qualified for is margin in the purchase price. Holding a fixed rate is margin against your payment moving. Keeping reserves sized to real failures is margin against a repair. Maintaining the property is margin against the slow decline that thins your tenant pool and your rent. Four different decisions, one underlying property: room between what the situation demands and what you can supply.

That framing explains something owners find counterintuitive. Almost every forced sale is a structural shortfall that a market event exposed — the market rarely causes the loss on its own. Two owners in the same neighborhood, in the same downturn, with the same value decline, end up in completely different places depending on whether they had room. One waits it out; the other lists in the worst quarter of the cycle.

Which means the protection is bought at the beginning and cannot be assembled later. Once the payment is set, the loan type is chosen, and the reserve is whatever it is, your ability to hold is largely determined. If you are deciding between a better property and more margin, the margin is usually the better asset.
