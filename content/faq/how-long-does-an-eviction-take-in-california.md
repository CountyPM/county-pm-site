An uncontested nonpayment eviction can move in a matter of weeks, but a contested one commonly takes four months or more from the first missed rent to getting the unit back. In our Ventura County experience, about four months is a realistic plan for a contested case, and longer if the tenant is coached or demands a jury.

The statutory pieces add up. A three-day notice runs three days excluding weekends and court holidays. After the lawsuit is served, the tenant has ten court days to respond. If the case is contested, the court is supposed to try it within 20 days of a request to set the case for trial, but calendars, continuances, motions, and jury demands routinely stretch that. After judgment comes the sheriff's notice to vacate and the lockout date.

Timelines vary by county. What used to run six to eight weeks now routinely runs much longer, which is why screening before move-in and catching problems early during the tenancy matter more than they used to. A bankruptcy filing can extend the timeline further.
