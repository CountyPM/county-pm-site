Two. Whether the person is an individual with a disability, and what disability-related task the animal has been trained to perform. That is the entire permitted inquiry for a service animal.

What you cannot do is nearly everything owners instinctively reach for: you may not ask for a demonstration of the task, request documentation or medical records, or require certification, registration, an ID card, or a vest. California does not require a service animal to be professionally trained or certified — the handler may have trained it themselves — so there is no credential to check, and demanding one is itself a violation.

And if the disability and the animal's work are apparent — a guide dog with a visibly blind handler, for example — skip the two questions too. Once the need is obvious, asking anything is a violation. Approve and move on.

Note the distinction with emotional support animals, which run on different rules: for a support animal you may request documentation from a reliable third party meeting California's standard (a California-licensed provider, a 30-day client relationship, an individualized assessment). The two-question limit above is the service-animal rule. Deciding which kind of animal you're dealing with is the first step of every request, because it determines what you're allowed to ask.

This is general information, not legal advice; fair-housing rules carry real liability, so confirm a specific situation against the Civil Rights Department guidance above or with counsel.
