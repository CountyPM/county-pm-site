Oxnard added a regulatory layer roughly every two years and now runs one of the more demanding rental environments in Southern California. Ventura, seven miles up the coast, went through the same debate at the same time and reached the opposite conclusion.

That divergence is the most useful fact in this post. Two adjacent cities, one county, the same housing pressure, different answers. If you own in both — and plenty of our owners do — you are operating under two materially different rulebooks, and the differences are not marginal.

Here is Ventura's, in the same eight fields.

## 1. Rent regulation

There is no local rent cap in the City of Ventura.

The council considered one. After a multi-year process including community workshops and staff study, it declined to pursue rent stabilization in 2025, with members citing concern that further capping rents would reduce housing investment and worsen the city's supply problem. A rental registry was rejected in the same round.

So your ceiling is AB 1482 alone — five percent plus the regional Consumer Price Index, capped at ten percent — and only if you failed to properly claim the state exemption. A correctly noticed single-family home or condominium in Ventura has no rent ceiling at all.

Note how differently that works from Oxnard. There, the Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act is what saves your house from a local four percent cap. Here, there is no local cap to be saved from. The exemption analysis matters just as much, but it is doing different work — and getting it wrong costs you the same either way.

*The exemption is conditional and easily lost. See our separate post on claiming it, particularly the renewal trap.*

## 2. Just cause and termination

Ventura has no local just cause ordinance. State law governs.

The city adopted one once. Chapter 6.1010 was passed as an emergency ordinance on December 2, 2019, deliberately structured as a 30-day bridge to AB 1482, and it carried an express sunset terminating it January 1, 2020 unless extended. It was not extended.

That leaves AB 1482's framework: twelve months of continuous occupancy before just cause attaches, twenty-four months for the longest-tenured tenant where occupants have changed, and the state exemption available for properly noticed single-family homes and condominiums.

Compare Oxnard, where local just cause reaches houses and condominiums at thirty days of tenancy regardless of exemption status. Seven miles, and an entirely different termination analysis.

## 3. Registration, licensing, and fees

No rental registry. The council rejected one in the same 2025 round that declined rent stabilization, so there is no per-unit registration fee of the kind Oxnard charges.

Business licensing depends on use. Short-term vacation rentals unambiguously require a City of Ventura business license renewed annually — the code treats a short-term rental as a business and says so directly. Whether long-term residential rental activity requires a business license is worth confirming with the city's Business License unit for your specific situation.

This is the field where Ventura is genuinely lighter than its neighbors. Unglamorous, but a real operating advantage.

## 4. Inspection and enforcement posture

Ventura runs no proactive cyclical rental inspection program. Reading that as light enforcement would be a mistake, because Ventura's approach to tenant protection routed around inspection entirely.

The city funded the Housing Rights Center to provide direct legal representation to renters facing eviction, and directed staff to return seeking expanded funding — with the stated ambition, among some council members, of representation regardless of income.

Understand what that changes. An inspector responds to habitability. Subsidized tenant counsel changes the economics of every dispute you have. A tenant who would not otherwise have retained a lawyer now has one at no cost, in a proceeding where your paperwork gets read closely.

The practical consequence is not that you will be inspected. It is that your notices, your service, your security deposit accounting, and your maintenance records will be examined by someone whose job is to find the defect. Notice practice that survived for years because nobody contested it will not survive that.

This is Ventura's real enforcement mechanism, and it goes almost entirely undiscussed.

## 5. Short-term rentals

Ventura permits short-term vacation rentals under Chapter 6.455 of the municipal code, and the framework was recently overhauled. It is now inspection-gated, capped, and limited in how many permits one owner may hold.

**If you currently hold a permit, the renewal deadline is August 31, 2026, and permits not renewed by that date are canceled.** We have written separately on the renewal mechanics because the timing is immediate.

For anyone evaluating a purchase: permits are capped with a waiting list once the maximum issues, an owner is limited to two permits total after a five-year transition with a maximum of one in Pierpont, and properties with an accessory dwelling unit cannot obtain a short-term permit for either the primary dwelling or the accessory unit. Advertising must display the city permit number. Operating without a permit is a misdemeanor.

Ventura is not hostile to short-term rentals. It is narrowing the field to permitted, inspected, professionally run operations — better for the operators who clear the bar, closed to everyone else.

## 6. Housing stock and operating economics

Ventura's rental stock is more heterogeneous than Oxnard's, and the differences are operationally real.

**Midtown and the older grid.** Pre-1960 construction, small lots, original systems. Charm that rents well and repairs poorly. Sewer laterals and electrical service are the recurring capital items.

**Hillside neighborhoods.** Slope, drainage, and access complicate everything from roof work to landscaping. The same job costs more here than on flat ground, every time.

**Pierpont and the beach tract.** Salt air accelerates every exterior system — fasteners, flashing, finishes, mechanical equipment. Maintenance intervals that work inland do not work here. This is also where short-term rental permit density is now expressly limited.

**Post-Thomas Fire rebuild stock.** The December 2017 fire destroyed hundreds of Ventura homes, and the rebuilt inventory is a cohort that exists nowhere else in the county: newer construction on established lots in established neighborhoods, at a basis reflecting when the owner rebuilt rather than when they bought. Current code compliance, modern systems, and for owners who held through the rebuild, a Proposition 13 basis that often did not reset. That combination is unusual and, for the right owner, strongly favorable to holding.

It also carries insurance exposure. Availability, pricing, and wildfire obligations in the wildland-urban interface are now a first-order variable in the hold-versus-sell calculation, not a line item. The city's own 2019 findings noted the fire had further strained an already low vacancy rate. The supply effect is durable.

## 7. Demand engine

Ventura's rental demand is broader-based and less cyclical than Oxnard's, and it comes from institutions rather than industries.

**Government.** Ventura is the county seat. County administration, the courts, and the surrounding professional ecosystem generate stable, credit-qualified tenancy that does not swing with a harvest or a deployment cycle.

**Healthcare.** Hospital and medical office concentration produces a steady stream of relocating clinical staff, often on multi-year contracts, often preferring to rent before buying. One of the more reliable applicant sources in the county.

**Education.** Ventura College and the institutional employment around it.

**Coastal premium.** Beach proximity supports rent levels inland comparables cannot reach, and draws a tenant who is choosing Ventura rather than settling for it. Retention runs better as a result.

The operating consequence: Ventura vacancy fills at higher rents with a more qualified pool than most of the county, but the pool is smaller. Overpricing here produces a longer vacancy than overpricing in Oxnard, because the deep, price-elastic demand underneath is not there to catch you.

## 8. Rent, hold, or sell in Ventura

**Toward holding.** No local rent cap. No rental registry. No local just cause. Institutional demand that does not swing. Coastal premium. And for a properly noticed single-family home, no rent ceiling at all — arguably the strongest regulatory position available anywhere in the county.

**Toward selling.** Wildfire insurance availability and cost, a genuine and worsening variable in the hillside and interface neighborhoods. Older stock with real capital needs. Subsidized tenant counsel that raises the cost of every dispute. And an anti-harassment ordinance still on the council's agenda.

**The question that actually decides it.** Ventura is currently the most owner-favorable regulatory environment among the county's larger cities. That is a real asset, and it is also a policy choice — the 2025 votes were not unanimous. Whether you hold depends less on today's rules than on whether your property's economics survive the version of Ventura that exists in ten years.

## What changed, what's pending

- **December 2019** — Emergency just cause ordinance adopted and allowed to sunset January 1, 2020. Its exemptions covered institutional facilities, government-owned units, resident-owner boarding, mobile homes, and transient occupancies. Single-family homes and condominiums were not exempt.
- **2022** — Ordinance adopted addressing substantial remodel evictions and relocation assistance.
- **2025** — Council declined rent stabilization and a rental registry.
- **2026** — Short-term vacation rental ordinance overhauled; existing permit renewal deadline August 31, 2026.
- **Pending** — An anti-harassment ordinance remains among the measures expected to return to council for a vote.
