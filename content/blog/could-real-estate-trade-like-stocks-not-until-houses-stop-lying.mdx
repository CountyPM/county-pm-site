Every so often someone asks me if real estate is heading toward the stock market — one property, many owners, a transaction closed in minutes instead of months. Fractional shares. Instant pricing. No waiting on an appraiser, a loan officer, or a thirty-day escrow.

Maybe someday. But not yet. And the reason isn't a technology gap. It's a judgment gap.

## A Judgment Gap, Not a Technology Gap

A share of stock is fungible. One share of a company is identical to every other share of that company, priced instantly because there's nothing to inspect — the asset is fully known the moment the market opens. A house is not that. Two homes on the same block, same square footage, same year built, can be worth wildly different amounts depending on things a spreadsheet will never surface.

## Every House Lies Until Someone Checks

A listing photo and a square footage number are data. They are not the truth about a property. You find out what a house actually is when you're standing in the crawl space, or a tenant mentions the water heater's been making a noise, or you notice the electrical panel is original to a 1962 build and nobody's touched it since. Automated pricing models assume a house is knowable from the outside. It isn't. Someone has to go look, and that someone needs to know what they're looking at.

## Condition Is a Moving Target, Not a Snapshot

A stock's value doesn't change because someone's been neglecting the roof for three years. A house's does — constantly — and the neglect isn't always visible from a drive-by or even a standard inspection. It takes someone who's seen a hundred variations of "this looks fine" turn into "this wasn't fine" to catch it before the money moves.

## Title and Disclosure Are the Risk, Not Friction

California disclosure law, easements, liens, deferred maintenance disputes, tenant rights that ride along with the property at sale — none of that is friction waiting to be engineered away. That *is* the risk being priced. A minutes-not-months closing only works when the risk is already fully known and standardized. Real estate risk is local, specific, and it mostly surfaces under scrutiny — from someone who knows the property type, the jurisdiction, and what questions to ask.

## Fractional Ownership Solves Financing, Not Judgment

Fractional ownership, to be fair, solves a real problem — financing. You can split ownership of a building into shares; Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) have done it for decades. What you can't split off is the responsibility for knowing whether the building was worth buying into in the first place, or whether it's being managed well after the fact. Many owners, one piece, still needs someone accountable for the piece being sound.

## Local Knowledge Doesn't Scale — That's Why It Matters

And that's really the piece that doesn't scale — local knowledge — which is exactly why it still holds value. A national pricing model can tell you what a three-bedroom "should" sell for based on comparable sales. It can't tell you that a particular street floods in an atmospheric river, or that a Homeowners Association (HOA) has a reserve funding problem nobody's disclosed yet, or that a landlord has a pattern of deferred repairs the tenant complaints already hint at. That's not information that gets better with more data. It's earned, one property and one bad surprise at a time.

## Getting to the Ballpark vs. Getting on Base

There's a version of the future where more of this gets automated — faster comps, better data pipelines, more of the paperwork handled by machines instead of people. Some of that is coming, and some of it should. But automation gets you to the ballpark. It gives you a number that's close enough for a homeowner checking their equity, or an investor scanning a market. It doesn't get you on base.

Getting on base still takes someone who's done this long enough to know what a house isn't telling you — and knowing when to bring in someone who does. If you're an owner trying to decide whether to hold, sell, or bring in help managing what you have, reach out — and if what you need is a trusted agent on the sales side, I can point you to someone who's earned that trust the same way: by getting it right, one property at a time.
