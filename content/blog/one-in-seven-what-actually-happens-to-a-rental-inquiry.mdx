An owner asked me last month why it takes so long to fill a unit when the listing "got a ton of interest." Fair question. The answer is that interest and tenants are very different things, and the distance between them is the whole job.

## A hundred inquiries was never a hundred prospects

Here's the shape of it, drawn from our own numbers across seven years. For every hundred inquiries a listing generates, roughly fourteen become an actual application — about one in seven. The other six of seven evaporate: wrong price, wrong timing, wrong pets, wrong commute, or just someone who was browsing on their lunch break and never meant to move.

That's not a leak to be plugged. That's the filter doing its work. A hundred inquiries was never a hundred prospects.

Of the applications that do come in, screening runs about one and a half to two background and credit checks apiece — because a household is often more than one adult, and every adult gets checked. So the funnel narrows again, and it should.

## The number that isn't at the top or the bottom

The number I want owners to see isn't at either end. It's the middle — the showing. And the showing is the part that doesn't reduce to a statistic, because it's where I stop processing leads and start matching a person to a place.

Here's what that looks like in practice. Someone books a showing for a specific unit. I meet them there. In ten minutes of conversation I usually learn what they actually need — which is frequently not the unit they called about. So depending on my schedule, I take them to another property that fits better. Sometimes it's a second appointment. And sometimes I tell them not to sign anything yet, because I've got a listing coming in a few weeks that suits them, and they should wait for it.

That's the part no funnel captures. A booked showing for one property can end with a lease on a different one — or on a unit that isn't even listed yet. The record for the first property just shows a showing that "didn't convert." On paper it looks like a miss. In reality it's the match working.

## Why this matters to an owner

The temptation — especially for an owner filling their own unit — is to treat leasing as a volume problem. Get more eyeballs, get more applications, take the first qualified one. But the eyeballs were never the constraint. The judgment in the middle is.

The funnel can show you how a hundred inquiries thin to a handful of applicants. It can't show you whether the applicant you took was the right one. That part is still done by a person who knows the inventory and asks the right question at the showing.
