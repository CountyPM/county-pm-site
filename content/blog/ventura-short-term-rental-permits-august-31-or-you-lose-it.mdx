If you hold a short-term vacation rental permit in the City of Ventura, stop reading everything else and confirm your renewal status today.

The city has overhauled its short-term vacation rental ordinance. Existing permit holders are grandfathered into the renewal process — but only if they renew by **August 31, 2026**. The city has characterized this as a formal 60-day notice, and states that permits not renewed by the deadline will be canceled.

Not suspended. Canceled. And with a permit cap and waiting list structure now in place, a canceled permit is not something you simply reapply for.

## What renewal requires

- **Renewal fee:** $1,526
- **Inspection fee:** $574
- **Total:** $2,100, at fees effective July 1, 2026
- **A code enforcement inspection you must pass** before the permit issues
- **A current City of Ventura business license** — verify before you apply, not during
- **At least $1,000 in annual transient occupancy tax paid** to be eligible

Renewal runs through Ventura Online Permit Services, a new process for most operators. Budget time for the unfamiliar system, and budget more for the inspection. That is the step that can fail, and there is limited runway left to correct a problem and re-inspect.

## Three structural changes worth understanding

**A permit cap with a waiting list.** New applications opened July 13, 2026, and no waiting list is in effect yet. One is established once the city issues its maximum number of permits. The window for entering this market is open now, and it will close.

**A two-permit ceiling per owner.** Five years from the ordinance's effective date, an owner may hold only two permits total, with a maximum of one in Pierpont. If your model assumed a portfolio of Ventura short-term rentals, that model has an expiration date.

**Accessory dwelling units are out entirely.** Where a property has an accessory dwelling unit, both the primary dwelling and the accessory unit may only be rented for terms longer than 30 days. No short-term permit issues for either one. Building the accessory unit does not add a short-term rental. It eliminates the one you had.

That last point is the third time in recent months we have written some version of the same sentence. An accessory dwelling unit is a durable improvement to cash flow and a serial destroyer of regulatory positions. It costs you the AB 1482 exemption on the main house. In Ventura, it costs you short-term rental eligibility outright. Model it before you build it.

## What to do today

1. **Confirm your business license is current.** This is the most common disqualifier and the easiest to overlook.
2. **Check your transient occupancy tax history** against the $1,000 annual minimum.
3. **Start the renewal in Ventura Online Permit Services now**, not the last week of August.
4. **Walk the property against fire, building, and safety compliance** before the inspector does.
5. **Verify every detail above directly with the City.** Fee schedules and deadlines move, and the consequence of relying on a stale figure here is losing the permit.

## The strategic read

Ventura is not banning short-term rentals. It is professionalizing them — inspection-gated, capped, and limited to two per owner. That produces a market with fewer operators, higher barriers to entry, and better economics for whoever remains inside the fence.

The owners who lose access will mostly be the ones who missed a date.
