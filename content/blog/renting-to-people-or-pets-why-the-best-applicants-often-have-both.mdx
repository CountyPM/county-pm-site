## "No pets" shrinks your pool

Every applicant you rule out lowers your odds of finding the strongest one, and raises the odds your home sits empty. Vacancy is the costliest risk you face: every empty month is income you never get back.

## It doesn't keep animals out anyway

Under the federal Fair Housing Act and California's Fair Employment and Housing Act, assistance animals, including service animals and emotional support animals, aren't pets. You must reasonably accommodate them even under a no-pets policy, with no pet rent and no pet deposit. A no-pets rule mostly gives up the chance to screen pets on your terms and get paid for the added wear.

## Screen the person, then the animal

Credit, income, and rental history show how an applicant handles obligations, including the home and the pet. Pet damage follows patterns you can screen for:

- **Dogs:** Chewing and urine damage cluster at both ends of life. Puppies chew and have accidents until trained, and senior dogs can lose control near the end. A mature, house-trained adult is usually the lowest risk. Ask the age, ask about training, and get a prior landlord's reference.
- **Cats:** Most use a litter box by instinct. The exception is spraying, which is territorial marking, mostly by male cats and far more common in cats that aren't neutered. Ask whether the cat is spayed or neutered and whether it's indoor-only.

## Put the protection in writing

- **A pet addendum:** name each animal, prohibit additional ones, and spell out responsibility for flooring, odor, and flea treatment at move-out.
- **Renter's insurance with liability coverage,** especially for dogs.
- **Periodic inspections** to catch small problems early.
- **Homeowners association rules:** check them first, since some restrict pets regardless of the owner's preference.

## The payoff: faster rent-up, more money

A wider pool leases faster and lets you pick the strongest applicant. Pet rent of $50 to $100 a month per animal adds income on top of the base rent, separate from the deposit. And tenants with pets tend to stay longer, because pet-friendly rentals are hard to find. That means fewer turnovers and fewer vacancies. Screen well, price the risk, and a pet-friendly policy fills your rental sooner and pays you more every month.
