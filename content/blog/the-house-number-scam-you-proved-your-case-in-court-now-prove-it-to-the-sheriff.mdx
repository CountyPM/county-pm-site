You served proper notice, filed the complaint, proved up your case, and walked out with a judgment and a writ of possession. You'd think the hard part was over.

There's a new trick circulating among tenants facing eviction that forces you to prove your case a second time, not to a judge but to a sheriff's deputy standing at the curb. It costs the tenant nothing but a can of paint. I haven't run into it in my own practice yet. After 50 years in Ventura County landlord-tenant work, though, I'd rather you be ready before the deputy shows up.

## How the Scam Works

Every document in an eviction, from the notice through the complaint, judgment, and writ of possession, identifies the property by street address. When the sheriff's deputy arrives to carry out the lockout, the house number is what they check.

So the tenant repaints the curb number from 400 to 404 and swaps or removes the numbers on the house. The deputy arrives expecting 400 and sees 404.

## Why Deputies Walk Away

Locking out the wrong household is a serious liability for the deputy and the county. When the address doesn't match the paperwork, the safe move is to leave and let the landlord reschedule. That can mean weeks of additional lost rent.

The Assessor's Parcel Number (APN) and legal description that pin down the property everywhere else in real estate don't help at the curb.

## It's Not Legal, but That Won't Save Your Lockout Date

California Fire Code Section 505 requires address numbers to be visible from the street, and most cities have similar ordinances. Deliberately altering them to mislead an officer may also cross into obstruction.

None of that matters on lockout day, though. What matters is whether you can prove to the deputy, on the spot, that this is the right house.

## Your Lockout-Day Evidence Kit

- Dated move-in and inspection photos showing the original number on the house
- A Google Street View printout of the property
- The county assessor's parcel map with the lot marked
- The addresses of the neighbors on each side, since a fake number usually breaks the street's sequence
- A key that fits the current lock
- Advance notice to the sheriff's civil unit that the address may be tampered with

Be there in person, or have your property manager there. Plan to meet the deputy yourself, since your presence is your chance to make the case.

## The Takeaway

Treat lockout day as a second hearing with a very short trial. The deputy is the judge, the property is the courtroom, and the verdict comes in about two minutes. Bring your evidence the same way you did for court.

Start building that file at move-in, with inspection photos that show the house number in frame, and you'll win the second round as surely as the first.
