# THE "CHEAP RENT" TRAP


You're angry about the rent increase. That anger is worth
something — if you know how to use it.

Here's the uncomfortable truth: cheap rent doesn't save you.
It sedates you. When your rent is artificially low — frozen by
rent control or just a landlord who hasn't kept up — you stop
feeling the pressure. And without pressure, most people don't move.

Meanwhile, the inflation machine that's been running since 1971
quietly erodes whatever you're saving. The house you keep saying
you'll "buy someday" gets more expensive every quarter. And you
stay comfortable. And comfortable tenants stay tenants.

> "When your rent starts to look like a mortgage payment, you have
> to ask: Am I building equity for myself — or for someone else?"

The rent increase is not the enemy. It's the alarm clock. It's
the market telling you that time is no longer on your side — and
if you don't act, the gap between where you are and where you
want to be grows wider, not narrower.

The difference between a tenant and an owner isn't a bank
balance. It's the decision to stop accepting "cheap" and start
building something real.

## THREE MOVES TO MAKE RIGHT NOW

### 1. KILL THE 20% MYTH.

The 20% down payment is a relic. With an FHA 203(b) loan, you
can enter the market with as little as 3.5% down. That changes
the math entirely — run the numbers before you dismiss the idea.

### 2. POOL YOUR WAY IN.

If you can't do it alone, don't. Find one or two people you
trust, buy a multi-family property together, live in one unit,
and let the rental income from the others carry the mortgage.
It's been done for a hundred years. It still works.

### 3. THE LEGACY MOVE — FOR PARENTS.

Don't let your kids save for a decade while the market rises
50%. Help them with the entry cost now. Let them build equity
young. The compounding starts immediately — and so does your
family's wealth trajectory.

---

Every month you spend in "wait and see" mode is a month of
equity you didn't build, appreciation you didn't capture, and a
mortgage you paid for someone else's retirement instead of your own.

The path from tenant to owner isn't a miracle. It's a plan,
executed one decision at a time. The ladder is right here.
But it keeps climbing — with or without you.
